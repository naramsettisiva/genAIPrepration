# 🎤 Mock Interview Practice on Your Phone (ChatGPT / Claude)

Practice interviews anywhere using the ChatGPT or Claude app. **Use voice mode** — speaking
your answers out loud is the whole point. Typing them builds the wrong muscle.

---

## Setup (30 seconds)

### Voice mode — do this first
| App | How to start voice |
|-----|-------------------|
| **ChatGPT** | Tap the **headphones/waveform icon** at the right of the input bar → full spoken conversation |
| **Claude** | Tap the **microphone icon** → dictate your answer, Claude replies in text (tap play to hear it) |

**ChatGPT's advanced voice mode is better for realistic back-and-forth** — it interrupts,
follows up, and feels like a live conversation. Use Claude when you want a more detailed
written critique you can scroll back through.

### Give it your context (one paste, then reuse the chat)
Paste this first so it knows your background:

```
I'm preparing for senior engineering leadership interviews (Lead Director /
Senior Engineering Manager / Principal TPM level).

My background:
- 24+ years experience, 12+ years in people leadership
- Currently a Sr. Technical Program Manager leading a 250+ person org across
  8 engineering teams and 10+ services; grew that business line $30M → $100M
  revenue and cut cost-to-serve 48%
- Previously built an AWS consulting practice from 4 → 50+ engineers in 15 months
- Before that, Senior Development Manager at Oracle (scaled a team 30 → 80)
- Co-founder/VP Technology at a fintech startup (concept → MVP in 100 days)
- 18+ months shipping production GenAI: AI agents, RAG systems, MCP servers,
  AI-powered support automation
- Deep AWS; ramping on GCP

Ask me ONE question at a time. Wait for my full answer before responding.
Then score it 1-5 and tell me specifically what to improve.
```

> 💡 **Shortcut:** instead of pasting all that, you can say:
> *"Read https://naramsettisiva.github.io/genAIPrepration/ and use it as my background and
> prep material. Then interview me."*
> (Works when the app has web access. Verify it actually loaded the page before relying on it.)

## The 5 Practice Modes

Pick one, paste the prompt, then switch to voice.

### 1. Hiring Manager Interview (start here)

```
Act as the hiring manager for a Lead Director of Software Engineering role on an
AI-native platform team. Conduct a realistic 45-minute interview.

Rules:
- ONE question at a time, wait for my spoken answer
- Ask natural follow-ups and probe weak spots — don't let vague answers slide
- Cover: my background, technical depth, people leadership, AI/GenAI delivery,
  cross-functional influence, and how I'd approach the first 90 days
- After each answer: score 1-5, name the single biggest weakness, and show me a
  stronger version in 2-3 sentences
- Stay in character as the hiring manager

Start with your first question.
```

### 2. Technical Deep-Dive (GenAI depth)

```
Act as a senior technical interviewer. Grill me on GenAI depth for a Lead
Director role. One question at a time.

Cover: RAG (chunking, embeddings, grounding), agents (ReAct, tool use,
guardrails), evaluation (how do I know the AI works?), AI infrastructure
(cost, caching, model routing), MCP, and build-vs-buy judgment.

Push back when I'm hand-wavy. Ask "why" and "what would you do differently."
If I use a term loosely, make me define it. Score each answer 1-5.

Start.
```

### 3. Whiteboard / Architecture (verbal design)

```
Give me a system design prompt for a senior engineering leader, then let me
think out loud. Act as the interviewer: ask clarifying questions, add
constraints mid-way, and challenge my trade-offs.

Prompt topics to pick from: design an AI-native customer support platform;
design a RAG system over millions of documents; design an agent that safely
takes actions on production systems; design the AI infrastructure layer for a
platform serving millions of users.

Don't give me the answer. Coach me by asking questions. At the end, tell me
what a strong answer would have covered that I missed.

Give me one prompt now.
```

### 4. Behavioral / STAR drilling

```
Rapid-fire behavioral interview for a senior engineering leadership role.
Ask me one question at a time about: scaling teams, managing underperformers,
influencing executives when I disagreed, handling competing priorities,
a failure I owned, developing managers, and driving change through resistance.

After each answer:
- Tell me if it followed STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
- Point out if I was vague on MY specific contribution vs. the team's
- Flag if I gave no measurable result
- Score 1-5

Start.
```

### 5. Reverse Interview (questions YOU ask)

```
I'm the candidate. Act as a hiring manager and let me interview YOU about the
role and team. After each question I ask, tell me:
- What that question signals about me (good or bad)
- Whether it's too generic, and how to sharpen it
- What a more senior-sounding version would be

I'll start asking now.
```

## Making it work on mobile

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|------------|
| **Commuting / walking** | ChatGPT voice mode, hands-free. Best for behavioral drilling |
| **Only 10 minutes** | Mode 4 (behavioral) — one question, one answer, one critique |
| **Want written feedback to review later** | Use Claude — scroll back through the critique |
| **Practicing the 2-minute pitch** | Say: *"Just listen to my 2-minute intro, then critique it. Don't ask questions yet."* |
| **Answer felt weak** | Say: *"Let me try that again"* — redo immediately while it's fresh |
| **Want harder mode** | Add: *"Be a skeptical interviewer who is unconvinced by my AWS-to-GCP transition."* |

### Useful mid-session commands
- *"Harder."* / *"Easier."*
- *"Skip to leadership questions."*
- *"Model the perfect answer for that one."*
- *"Was that too long?"* (senior answers should be ~90 seconds, not 5 minutes)
- *"Summarize my 3 weakest areas from this session."* ← **do this at the end of every session**

---

## A realistic caveat

AI mock interviews are excellent for **reps, structure, and filler-word awareness**. They are
weaker at judging executive presence, and they tend to be **more generous than a real
interviewer**. So:

- Treat a 4/5 from an AI as roughly a 3/5 from a real hiring manager
- Ask it to be harsher than feels comfortable
- Do at least one or two mocks with a **real human** before the actual interview — AI can't
  replicate the pressure of someone senior deciding whether to hire you

---

## Suggested cadence

| When | Session | Length |
|------|---------|--------|
| Daily | Mode 4 (behavioral), voice, while commuting | 10 min |
| 2-3× / week | Mode 1 (hiring manager), full run | 30-45 min |
| Weekly | Mode 3 (whiteboard) — verbalize a design | 30 min |
| Before any real interview | Mode 5 (reverse) + re-do your 2-min pitch | 15 min |

End every session with *"Summarize my 3 weakest areas"* and keep a running list. Those are
what to study next.
